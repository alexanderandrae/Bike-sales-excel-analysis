# Bike Sales Excel Analysis.

## Project Overview. 
This project analyses bike sales data in excel to identify customer patterns, purchasing behavior, and what factors are associated with bike purchases. 

## Business Question.
Which customer segments are the most likely to purchase bikes, and what patterns could help the business to improve targeting and sales decisions? 

## Tools Used. 
- Excel.
- Data cleaning.
- Pivot tables.
- Charts.
- Dashboarding.

## Workflow. 
1. Cleaned and standardized the raw data.
2. Revieved customer and sales variables.
3. Built pivot table summaries.
4. Created charts and dashboard views.
5. Interpreted findings in business terms to answer the overarching questions.

## Key findings. 
1. Single customers were the strongest buyer segment.
Single customers bought bikes at a 54.1% purchase rate (250 buyers from 462 customers), compared with 42.9% for married customers (231 buyers from 538 customers). Despite representing a smaller overall group, singles generated more purchases, suggesting they were the more responsive segment in this dataset.
2. The pacific region showed the highest purchase propensity, while North America underperformed on conversion. 
The Pacific region delivered the strongest purchase rate at 58.9% (113 buyers from 192 customers). Europe was relatively balanced at 49.3%, while North America had the largest customer base but a weaker purchase rate of 43.3% (220 buyers from 508 customers). This suggests North America offers scale, but not the strongest conversion efficiency.
3. Education level helped distinguish higher- and lower-propensity customer groups.
Customers with a Bachelor’s degree had the highest volume high performing segment, with a 55.2% purchase rate (169 buyers from 306 customers), followed closely by Graduate Degree customers at 54%. At the other end, Partial High School customers had a much lower purchase rate of 26.3% (20 buyers from 76 customers), making this the weakest performing education segment in the dataset.

## Recommendations. 
1. Prioritise single customers in campaign targeting and product positioning.
Since single customers showed the strongest response, future marketing should be designed around the needs of this segment first. Messaging should focus on convenience, independence, commuting efficiency, and lifestyle value, while married customers may need a different value proposition to improve conversion.
2. Use regional strategy differently: scale in North America, efficiency in Pacific.
The Pacific region appears to be the most efficient market for generating sales, so it is a strong candidate for concentrated campaign spend. North America, however, should not simply receive more volume; it needs better segmentation and more targeted messaging to improve conversion from its large customer base.
3. Segment educational groups instead of using one broad message for all customers.
Customers with Bachelor’s and Graduate qualifications were more likely to purchase, so premium or performance oriented messaging may be more effective for those groups. Lower converting education segments, especially Partial High School, may respond better to simpler value based messaging focused on affordability, practicality, and ease of use.

## Limitations. 
This analysis identifies **patterns and associations** in the data, but it does not prove causation. The dashboard is useful for exploratory analysis and segment comparison, but further work would be needed to test whether these patterns hold in a live commercial setting.
